Recruitment Demand Among Japanese Companies in Vietnam at the End of 2026: Labor Market Trends and Hiring Challenges

A Growing Recruitment Market and Increasing Competition for Talent

As 2026 draws to a close, Vietnam's labor market continues to show positive recruitment momentum. Many Japanese companies are strengthening their hiring activities to support business expansion, production requirements, and workforce planning for 2027.

However, attracting qualified professionals with strong technical expertise, practical experience, and Japanese language proficiency remains a significant challenge for many Japanese businesses.

1. Vietnam's Recruitment Market Continues to Grow

According to ManpowerGroup's Employment Outlook Survey for Q4 2026, 52% of employers in Vietnam plan to increase their workforce, while 16% expect to reduce headcount. Vietnam's Net Employment Outlook (NEO) stands at 36%, indicating a positive recruitment outlook.

In addition, according to JETRO's FY2025 Survey on Business Conditions of Japanese Companies Operating Overseas, 67.5% of Japanese companies in Vietnam expected to record profits in 2025. However, 48.2% reported that recruitment had become more difficult in recent years.

These figures highlight two parallel trends: Japanese companies continue to see business opportunities in Vietnam, while competition for qualified talent is becoming increasingly intense.

2. Key Industries and Positions with Strong Recruitment Demand

At the end of 2026, Japanese companies in Vietnam are expected to maintain recruitment demand across several key sectors.

IT & Technology

  • IT Project Managers (PM), Bridge System Engineers (BrSE), Business Analysts (BA).

  • Software Engineers, IT Managers.

  • Technical Pre-Sales Engineers, Project Management Officers (PMO).

Engineering & Manufacturing

  • Mechanical Engineers, Electrical Engineers.

  • Production Managers, Quality Control / Quality Assurance (QC/QA).

  • Manufacturing Engineers, Factory Managers.

Logistics & Supply Chain Management (SCM)

  • Logistics Managers, Supply Chain Managers.

  • Purchasing / Procurement Managers.

  • Import–Export Specialists, Warehouse Managers.

Sales & Management

  • Sales Managers, Business Development Managers.

  • Marketing Managers, Account Managers.

  • General Managers, HR Managers, Chief Accountants.

Candidates with experience working for Japanese companies, Japanese language proficiency, and an understanding of Japanese business culture often have a competitive advantage in the recruitment process.

3. Key Recruitment Challenges Facing Japanese Companies

Despite increasing hiring demand, Japanese companies continue to face several challenges:

  • Shortage of qualified talent: Finding candidates who combine professional expertise, practical experience, and language skills is difficult.

  • Intensifying salary competition: Experienced professionals have more employment opportunities across multinational and foreign-invested companies.

  • Longer recruitment processes: Management, IT, and specialized engineering positions often require multiple interview stages and extensive candidate assessments.

  • Employee retention challenges: Candidates increasingly value not only compensation and benefits but also career development and long-term growth opportunities.

To address these challenges, companies should develop recruitment plans in advance, review compensation packages against market benchmarks, and expand their candidate networks through professional recruitment partners.
