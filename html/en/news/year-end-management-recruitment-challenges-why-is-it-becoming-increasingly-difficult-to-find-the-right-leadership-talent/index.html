YEAR-END MANAGEMENT RECRUITMENT CHALLENGES: WHY IS IT BECOMING INCREASINGLY DIFFICULT TO FIND THE RIGHT LEADERSHIP TALENT?

Vietnam's Q4 2026 recruitment market: Hiring demand remains strong, but experienced management professionals are increasingly difficult to secure.

1. Rising recruitment demand towards the end of the year

The fourth quarter is a critical period for business operations and human resource planning in Vietnam. Companies are focused on achieving annual business targets, preparing budgets, developing strategies and planning their workforce requirements for the following year.

Demand for management professionals, including General Managers, Sales Managers, Business Development Managers, Production Managers, Supply Chain Managers, HR Managers and IT Managers, often becomes more urgent as companies expand their operations or restructure their organisations.

According to the ManpowerGroup Employment Outlook Survey Q4 2026, conducted among 285 employers in Vietnam:

  • 52% of employers expect to increase their workforce during Q4 2026.

  • 16% anticipate a decrease in headcount.

  • 32% plan to maintain their current workforce size.

These figures indicate continued recruitment demand towards the end of the year. However, increased hiring demand does not necessarily mean that companies can easily find suitable candidates, particularly for management and leadership positions.

2. A shortage of qualified middle and senior management talent

One of the most significant challenges in Vietnam's labour market is the shortage of professionals with proven management experience.

According to Reeracoen Vietnam's Vietnam Employer Hiring Study 2026:

  • 39% of employers report difficulties finding mid-level managers.

  • 53% identify shortages of technical and specialised skills as a major recruitment challenge.

  • 86% identify rising candidate salary expectations as their biggest hiring challenge.

Hard-to-fill positions are found across multiple industries, including:

  • Sales Manager / Business Development Manager.

  • Production Manager / Factory Manager.

  • Supply Chain Manager / Logistics Manager.

  • Engineering Manager.

  • HR Manager.

  • IT Project Manager.

For these positions, employers need more than technical expertise. Candidates must also demonstrate team leadership, operational management, problem-solving capabilities and the ability to contribute directly to business performance.

This is one of the main reasons why management recruitment typically takes longer than hiring for standard operational or administrative positions.

3. Three reasons why year-end management recruitment becomes more challenging

3.1. Highly qualified candidates are not actively looking for new opportunities

According to Adecco Vietnam Salary Guide 2026, based on research involving more than 10,000 professionals in Vietnam:

  • 26% are actively looking for new career opportunities.

  • 36% are open to new opportunities but are not actively searching.

  • 37% are not considering a job change.

This suggests that many experienced professionals are not necessarily visible on recruitment platforms or actively submitting applications.

Management professionals, in particular, tend to evaluate career changes carefully. They consider company stability, responsibilities, decision-making authority, career development opportunities and the overall compensation package.

Consequently, conventional job advertisements alone may not be sufficient to reach this talent segment.

3.2. A growing gap between recruitment budgets and candidate expectations

According to Reeracoen Vietnam's 2026 employer survey, 86% of employers consider rising salary expectations the biggest challenge in recruitment.

For management candidates, salary is not the only factor influencing a career move. Other important considerations include:

  • Performance-based and year-end bonuses.

  • 13th-month salary and employee benefits.

  • Decision-making authority and professional autonomy.

  • Career advancement and long-term development opportunities.

  • Corporate culture and management style.

Towards the end of the year, some candidates may prefer to remain with their current employers until they receive their Tet bonus or complete annual business objectives before considering a new position.

This can extend recruitment timelines and complicate salary negotiations.

3.3. Employers increasingly require multiple competencies in one candidate

For Japanese companies operating in Vietnam, management recruitment often involves additional requirements.

Beyond professional expertise, candidates may need to demonstrate:

  • Japanese or English communication skills.

  • Experience working for Japanese companies or multinational organisations.

  • The ability to manage Vietnamese employees.

  • Understanding of Japanese business practices and corporate culture.

  • Reporting, coordination and communication skills when working with headquarters in Japan.

A technically strong candidate may not necessarily have the ability to manage a bilingual team or coordinate effectively with Japanese executives.

The combination of professional expertise, management experience and foreign-language proficiency significantly narrows the available talent pool.

4. How should companies adapt their recruitment strategies?

To improve management recruitment outcomes towards the end of the year, companies should consider the following approaches:

First: Proactively approach candidates instead of relying solely on applications.

Use Executive Search and Direct Search strategies to identify and approach experienced professionals who are currently employed but may be suitable for new opportunities.

Second: Develop competitive compensation packages.

Benchmark salaries against current market data and consider total compensation, bonuses, benefits and long-term career opportunities.

Third: Define recruitment requirements clearly.

Distinguish essential qualifications from skills that can be developed after hiring. Excessive requirements can unnecessarily restrict the available candidate pool.

Fourth: Shorten recruitment and decision-making processes.

For highly qualified management candidates, lengthy interview processes and delayed feedback can result in losing talent to competing employers.

Fifth: Start workforce planning early for the following year.

Companies should not wait until key employees resign or operational issues arise before searching for replacements.
