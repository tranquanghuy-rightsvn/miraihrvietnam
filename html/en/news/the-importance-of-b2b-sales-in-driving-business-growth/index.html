The Importance of B2B Sales in Driving Business Growth

In a B2B (Business-to-Business) environment, Sales is much more than simply “selling” a product or service. It serves as a direct connection between a company and the market, identifying new business opportunities, building customer relationships, and converting customer needs into tangible revenue.

This role is particularly important in industries such as manufacturing, IT, logistics, trading, professional services, and enterprise solutions, where the capabilities of a B2B sales team can directly influence the speed of market expansion and long-term business growth.

B2B Sales Remains Essential in the AI Era

The rapid development of AI and digital channels is significantly changing the way B2B customers make purchasing decisions. However, this does not mean that the role of sales professionals is becoming less important.

According to a 2026 Gartner survey of 645 B2B buyers, 69% of buyers want sales representatives to validate information generated by AI. Buyers used an average of seven information sources in a recent purchase, while 45% had used generative AI to research vendors or products.

This indicates that the role of Sales is evolving from simply providing information to becoming a trusted advisor who validates information, reduces customer risk, and supports decision-making.

B2B Purchasing Decisions Often Involve Multiple Stakeholders

Unlike B2C transactions, B2B purchasing decisions are rarely made by a single individual.

According to Gartner, a B2B buying group may involve approximately 5 to 16 people across as many as four different functions. Gartner also found that 74% of surveyed buying teams experienced unhealthy conflict during the decision-making process. Meanwhile, buying groups that reached consensus were 2.5 times more likely to describe the deal as high quality.

Therefore, an effective B2B salesperson must do more than convince a single purchasing representative. They need to understand who will use the product, who influences the decision, who controls the budget, and who provides final approval.

This is why capabilities such as consultative selling, account management, negotiation, and stakeholder management are becoming increasingly important.

B2B Sales Directly Influences Business Growth

McKinsey research involving nearly 500 B2B companies found that top-performing companies in sales productivity generated approximately 2.5 times more gross margin per dollar invested in Sales than bottom-quartile companies.

Other McKinsey research has found that high-performing B2B sales organizations can achieve four to five times higher sales growth than lower-performing organizations. Approximately 85% of sales leaders surveyed also believed that solution selling would become a core sales capability.

These findings demonstrate that investing in Sales is not simply about hiring more salespeople.

More importantly, companies need sales teams capable of identifying the right customers, understanding their needs, designing appropriate solutions, and concentrating resources on high-value opportunities.

Customers Do Not Need More Salespeople — They Need Salespeople Who Understand Them

One of the most significant changes in today's B2B environment is that customers can independently access a vast amount of information before speaking with a salesperson.

According to Gartner, 73% of B2B buyers actively avoid suppliers that send irrelevant outreach.

A LinkedIn and Ipsos survey of 508 B2B buyers also found that 78% strongly agreed that sales outreach should be personalized.

As a result, traditional B2B sales approaches based primarily on mass emails, cold calls, or generic product presentations are becoming less effective.

Modern B2B sales professionals need to research customers before making contact: What challenges does the company face? How is its industry changing? Who are the decision-makers? Most importantly, what specific value can the proposed solution create for the customer?

B2B Sales Is the Bridge Between the Market and the Company

An effective B2B salesperson brings more than orders and revenue back to the company. They also provide valuable market intelligence.

Through direct interaction with customers, Sales can identify emerging needs, product feedback, competitor pricing, investment trends, reasons for winning or losing deals, and changes in customer purchasing behavior.

This information helps management adjust product strategy, pricing, marketing, and market-development plans.

For Japanese companies operating in Vietnam in particular, sales professionals who understand products, markets, business culture, languages, and customer decision-making processes can play a critical role in connecting the company with customers in Vietnam, Japan, and international markets.

The Future of B2B Sales: From “Salesperson” to “Business Partner”

AI can support lead generation, data analysis, sales email preparation, CRM management, and the automation of many administrative tasks.

However, the core value of B2B Sales is increasingly shifting toward activities that are more difficult to automate completely: understanding customer needs, building trust, proposing solutions, negotiating, and supporting complex purchasing decisions.

McKinsey's 2026 B2B Pulse research found that among growth-leading companies integrating AI into core sales processes, 59% reported benefits in sales productivity and 53% in customer experience.

Therefore, the key question for businesses today is no longer simply:

“Do we need B2B Sales?”

Instead, companies should be asking:

“What kind of B2B sales talent and capabilities do we need to achieve sustainable growth?”
