VIETNAM–JAPAN TRADE PROMOTION: RECENT DEVELOPMENTS AND FUTURE PROSPECTS

Strengthening Business Connections – Expanding Markets – Building Sustainable Economic Partnerships

1. Vietnam–Japan Economic Relations Enter a New Era

More than five decades after establishing diplomatic relations in 1973, Vietnam and Japan have developed a comprehensive partnership across trade, investment, manufacturing, technology, agriculture and human resource development.

The elevation of bilateral relations to a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership in November 2023 marked an important milestone, creating new opportunities for economic cooperation and commercial exchange between the two countries.

Japan remains one of Vietnam's most important trading and investment partners. Vietnam offers competitive manufacturing capabilities, a dynamic workforce, agricultural resources and a strategic position within regional supply chains. Japan, meanwhile, brings advanced technology, capital, sophisticated management expertise and extensive international distribution networks.

These complementary strengths provide a solid foundation for businesses from both countries to expand beyond traditional import-export activities into manufacturing partnerships, technology transfer, supply chain development and long-term investment.

2. Key Figures in Vietnam–Japan Bilateral Trade

According to Vietnam Customs and the Ministry of Industry and Trade, bilateral trade has continued to demonstrate strong growth.

Key trade indicators:

  • 2025 bilateral trade turnover: Approximately USD 51.43 billion, up 11.28% year-on-year, exceeding USD 50 billion for the first time.

  • Vietnam's exports to Japan: USD 26.77 billion, up 8.77%.

  • Vietnam's imports from Japan: USD 24.68 billion, up 14.13%.

  • Trade balance: Vietnam recorded a trade surplus of approximately USD 2.09 billion.

  • First nine months of 2026: Bilateral trade reached approximately USD 43.71 billion, up 15.34% year-on-year.

Vietnam's major exports to Japan include:

  • Textiles and garments.

  • Transport equipment and components.

  • Machinery, equipment and spare parts.

  • Wood and wood products.

  • Seafood and agricultural products.

  • Telecommunications and electronic products.

  • Footwear, coffee and processed food.

Notably, Vietnam's exports of wood and wood products to Japan reached approximately USD 2.15 billion in 2025, representing a 23.33% increase compared with the previous year.

In the opposite direction, Vietnam imports machinery, industrial equipment, electronic components, production materials and advanced technologies from Japan.

This complementary trade structure creates significant opportunities for businesses to develop long-term commercial partnerships and participate more deeply in each other's supply chains.

3. Major Vietnam–Japan Trade Promotion Activities in Recent Years

Trade promotion between Vietnam and Japan has become increasingly diversified, involving government agencies, trade promotion organizations, industry associations and private-sector businesses.

 International Trade Fairs, Exhibitions and Business Matching

International exhibitions, trade fairs and Business Matching programs remain essential channels for connecting companies and expanding market access.

Organizations such as the Vietnam Trade Promotion Agency (VIETRADE), the Japan External Trade Organization (JETRO), the ASEAN-Japan Centre (AJC) and various industry associations have supported business exchanges, product promotion and partner identification.

Key activities include:

  • Vietnamese companies participating in specialized exhibitions in Japan.

  • Japanese companies attending trade fairs and business events in Vietnam.

  • Vietnam–Japan business conferences and trade forums.

  • Direct B2B meetings between manufacturers, importers and distributors.

  • Trade missions and market research delegations between the two countries.

A notable example was the Vietnam–Japan Economic Cooperation Business Conference held in Tokyo on May 30, 2025. Organized by VIETRADE, the Vietnamese Trade Office in Japan and the ASEAN-Japan Centre, the event attracted more than 100 representatives and business participants from both countries.

These initiatives help companies identify potential customers, suppliers, distributors and investment partners through direct business engagement.

 Leveraging Free Trade Agreements (FTAs)

Free Trade Agreements are another important driver of bilateral trade.

Three key frameworks connecting Vietnam and Japan are:

  • VJEPA: Vietnam–Japan Economic Partnership Agreement.

  • AJCEP: ASEAN–Japan Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement.

  • CPTPP: Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership.

These agreements provide preferential tariff treatment, improve market access and create favorable conditions for trade and investment.

To maximize these benefits, businesses must understand rules of origin, Certificates of Origin (C/O), technical standards, quarantine regulations and product quality requirements.

Trade Promotion in Agriculture and Food Processing

Agriculture and food processing represent promising sectors for Vietnam–Japan cooperation.

Japan is a high-value market with sophisticated consumers and strict requirements concerning food safety, traceability, pesticide residues and product quality.

Vietnamese products with export potential include:

  • Coffee, cashew nuts and pepper.

  • Seafood and processed food.

  • Tropical fruits and vegetables.

  • Rice and rice-based products.

  • Legally sourced timber and certified wood products.

According to Vietnam's Ministry of Agriculture and Environment, Vietnamese exports of agricultural, forestry and fishery products to Japan increased from USD 3.17 billion in 2020 to USD 4.94 billion in 2025.

This growth demonstrates considerable opportunities for Vietnamese businesses to access Japanese importers, supermarket chains, food distributors and processing companies.

 Investment Promotion, Technology Transfer and Supply Chain Development

Beyond merchandise trade, investment and manufacturing cooperation are becoming increasingly important.

Japanese companies continue to explore opportunities in Vietnam in sectors such as:

  • Manufacturing and industrial processing.

  • Electronics and supporting industries.

  • Logistics and supply chain management.

  • Industrial plastics and materials.

  • High-tech agriculture.

  • Information technology and digital transformation.

  • Renewable energy and green manufacturing.

In particular, supply chain diversification and the China+1 strategy are creating additional opportunities for Vietnam to become an important manufacturing, sourcing and distribution hub for Japanese businesses.

Cooperation increasingly extends beyond factory establishment to include local supplier development, technology transfer, localization, productivity improvement and better corporate management.

4. Future Prospects for Vietnam–Japan Trade Cooperation

The outlook for bilateral trade and investment remains positive during the 2026–2030 period.

According to information published by Vietnam's Ministry of Agriculture and Environment in November 2026, Vietnam and Japan aim to increase bilateral trade turnover to USD 60 billion by 2030.

Several sectors are expected to offer significant opportunities.

Expanding Vietnamese Exports to Japan

Demand for high-quality food, processed agricultural products, wooden furniture, garments, electronic components and supporting industrial products will continue to create opportunities for Vietnamese exporters.

However, competition in Japan is determined not only by price but also by consistent quality, delivery reliability, product certification and supplier credibility.

Vietnamese businesses should therefore move beyond individual export orders and focus on developing long-term relationships with Japanese importers and distributors.

 Developing Supporting Industries and Supply Chains

Japanese manufacturers are increasingly interested in identifying and developing suppliers in Vietnam in areas such as industrial components, precision engineering, industrial plastics, packaging, materials and logistics services.

This creates opportunities for Vietnamese companies to participate more deeply in Japanese corporate supply chains, strengthen manufacturing capabilities and increase the value added of their products.

Promoting Digital Trade and Online Business Connections

Digital transformation is creating new approaches to international trade promotion.

B2B platforms, online business meetings, virtual exhibitions and digital supplier management systems enable businesses to identify partners more efficiently, reduce transaction costs and expand customer networks.

These solutions are particularly valuable for small and medium-sized enterprises seeking to enter the Japanese market without significant initial investment in physical commercial operations.

 Growing Demand for Japanese-Speaking and Highly Skilled Professionals

As bilateral trade and investment expand, demand is increasing for professionals who possess Japanese-language proficiency, an understanding of Japanese business culture and specialized expertise.

Key areas include:

  • International trade and business development.

  • Manufacturing and industrial engineering.

  • Logistics and Supply Chain Management (SCM).

  • Information Technology (IT).

  • Sales, procurement and project management.

  • Human resources and corporate management.

Professionals who can work effectively in multicultural environments play an essential role in connecting companies, implementing projects and maintaining long-term business relationships.

Human capital will therefore remain a critical factor in the success of Vietnam–Japan trade and investment cooperation.
