How to Negotiate Your Salary in an Interview with a Japanese Company?

1. Research the Market Salary

Before the interview, check the typical salary range for the position.

Consider factors such as years of experience, professional skills, Japanese proficiency, position level, and job responsibilities to determine a reasonable salary expectation.

2. Give a Salary Range

Instead of saying:

“My expected salary is VND 35 million.”

You can say:

“Based on my experience and the responsibilities of this position, I am expecting around VND 32–35 million per month. However, I am open to discussing the overall package.”

This gives both you and the employer more flexibility during the negotiation.

3. Explain Your Value

If you are asking for a higher salary, clearly explain what you can bring to the company.

Consider your: Experience + Professional Skills + Japanese Ability + Achievements + Potential Contribution

For example:

“I have JLPT N1, five years of B2B sales experience, and experience working directly with Japanese clients. Based on this background, I am expecting a salary of around VND 30–35 million per month.”

4. Don’t Focus Only on Base Salary

When receiving a job offer, consider the entire compensation package, including bonuses, allowances, insurance, incentives, annual leave, salary reviews, and career development opportunities.

A slightly lower base salary may still be attractive if the company offers better benefits and long-term career opportunities.

5. Don’t Reject a Lower Offer Immediately

For example, if you expect VND 35 million but the company offers VND 32 million, you could ask:

“If I achieve the agreed targets after the probation period, would the company consider reviewing my salary to VND 35 million?”

Successful salary negotiation is not simply about asking for the highest possible salary. The key is to demonstrate the value you can bring to the company and find a package that works for both sides.
