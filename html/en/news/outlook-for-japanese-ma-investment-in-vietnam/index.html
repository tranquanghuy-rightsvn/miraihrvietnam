Outlook for Japanese M&A Investment in Vietnam

In recent years, Vietnam has remained one of the key strategic markets for Japanese companies in Southeast Asia. In addition to greenfield investment, such as establishing new companies or manufacturing facilities, Mergers and Acquisitions (M&A) are becoming an increasingly important approach for Japanese companies seeking faster access to the Vietnamese market, distribution networks, human resources, and manufacturing capabilities.

Vietnam Continues to Attract Japanese Investment

According to Vietnam’s foreign investment authority under the Ministry of Finance, Vietnam attracted more than USD 38.4 billion in registered foreign investment in 2025, while disbursed FDI exceeded USD 27.6 billion, representing an increase of approximately 9% compared with the previous year.

Japan ranked fourth among countries and territories investing in Vietnam in 2025, with approximately USD 3.73 billion in registered investment capital, an increase of 6.6% year-on-year. In terms of accumulated investment, Japan remains one of Vietnam’s largest foreign investors.

Another notable indicator for the M&A market is that foreign investment through capital contributions and share acquisitions increased by 54.8% in 2025 compared with the previous year. This suggests that foreign investors are increasingly interested not only in establishing new projects but also in acquiring stakes in existing Vietnamese businesses.

Japanese Investors Maintain a Significant Presence in Vietnam’s M&A Market

According to KPMG’s Vietnam M&A 2025 report, Japanese investors accounted for approximately USD 214 million in disclosed M&A transaction value during the first ten months of 2025, representing around 9% of the disclosed transaction value among the investor groups covered by KPMG.

KPMG also noted that Japanese and South Korean investors have shown particular interest in industrial, manufacturing, and materials-related assets, reflecting the extensive supply-chain networks that companies from both countries have developed in Vietnam.

Meanwhile, PwC reported that Vietnam recorded approximately 60 completed M&A transactions by October 2025. Industrial and consumer & retail sectors each represented approximately 18% of transactions, while technology accounted for around 17%.

These developments indicate that Vietnam continues to offer opportunities for strategic investors seeking both market expansion and long-term business development.

Four Key M&A Trends for Japanese Companies in Vietnam

First, M&A will increasingly be used as a tool for faster market entry and expansion. Instead of spending several years building operations from the ground up, Japanese companies can acquire shares in or establish strategic partnerships with Vietnamese businesses that already have customers, licenses, employees, distribution networks, and local market knowledge.

Second, manufacturing and supply chains are expected to remain important investment areas. Japanese investors continue to show interest in industrial, manufacturing, and materials-related assets. The diversification of Asian supply chains may also encourage Japanese companies to identify Vietnamese partners with strong manufacturing capabilities, quality management systems, and the ability to participate in international supply chains.

Third, investment priorities are gradually shifting from cost advantages toward higher value-added activities. KPMG has observed that Vietnam’s next investment cycle is increasingly focused on areas such as technology, R&D, regional functions, and strategic capital allocation. Vietnamese companies possessing technology, management capabilities, data, established brands, or highly skilled teams may therefore attract greater attention from Japanese strategic investors.

Fourth, investors are becoming increasingly selective when evaluating M&A targets. Rather than focusing solely on growth, investors are paying greater attention to financial transparency, corporate governance, scalability, and business resilience. This trend reflects a more disciplined approach to allocating capital toward businesses with sound fundamentals and sustainable long-term value.

Opportunities for Vietnamese Companies

These trends create opportunities for Vietnamese companies seeking Japanese strategic partners, investment capital, technology, access to overseas markets, or potential business transfers and acquisitions.

However, companies seeking Japanese investors should prepare carefully. Important areas include financial statements, legal and tax compliance, shareholder structures, assets, employment contracts, intellectual property rights, and corporate governance.

Due diligence is generally an important stage of the M&A process before investors make final investment decisions.

Differences in language, business culture, management practices, and decision-making processes between Vietnamese and Japanese companies may also make partner searches and negotiations more complex. A business partner capable of bridging the two markets can therefore play an important role in identifying potential partners and facilitating initial discussions.
